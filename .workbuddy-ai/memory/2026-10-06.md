## Session 3 — the prover's whistle, generalisation and callee partition (commit `81c0fbb`)

SCP4 1999 §4's *second* named theorem ("correctness of binary tree reversals")
diverged: 6,137 leaves at `--steps 10000`, 123,291 at `--steps 200000`. Two
mechanisms landed, both in `refal-core` and both gated:

1. **The whistle + generalisation.** `whistle_ancestor` fires when an ancestor
   *embeds* in the pair AND the pair is **not an instance** of it. **The
   instance exclusion is load-bearing**: a split's branch is always an instance
   of the configuration it came from, so a whistle firing on embedding alone
   fires at the first split and proves nothing. `sequence_is_instance_of` had to
   be written from scratch — `match_symbolic_pattern` is deliberately
   three-valued and returns `Unknown` for a call, which is useless for a purely
   structural question. The generalised claim is proved **inline**, and only a
   complete proof of it closes the branch: a refutation of a *stronger* claim
   says nothing about this one, so anything less is `Stuck`. A generalisation
   that is only a renaming is refused (the driver's own answer for that case).
2. **The callee-driven partition.** `branches_for` splits by the callee's own
   sentence patterns when a side is blocked by a call whose only argument is the
   blocked variable — `SplitStrategy::Pattern` read at the level of an equation.
   This was the **actual** blocker: the three-way `[]`/`s.H e.T`/`(e.B) e.T`
   split generated branches `Rev` can never accept.

**Result:** `examples/equiv-tree-reversal.ref` terminates — 73 steps,
`complete: yes`, `(Leaf)` decided by reflexivity — where it previously diverged.
The `Node` branch is `open`, and the reason is located: unfolding it gives a pair
with **two independent components** and the prover drives the pair as one
sequence rather than a conjunction of sub-goals. **Next: generalise the pair,
not each side.** No regression: assoc still proved, wrong pair still refuted,
the soundness gate green.
